## RT0 cuboid under an imposed field

<!-- id: S2.rt0.p1 -->
The case is a homogeneous copper cuboid of 1×1×2 mm at 200 kHz, with imposed Bz=1 mT RMS and zero net transport. Three-component current is represented by face-flux degrees of freedom, with separate continuity, port-constraint, and imposed-field power checks. At quadrature q=14, meshes of 8192, 16000, and 27648 cells give losses of 0.927463, 0.933539, and 0.936927 mW. Consecutive refinement changes are 0.655090% and 0.362916%, below the 1% target. Increasing the finest case to q=18 changes loss and complex face flux relatively by 1.55×10⁻⁸ and 1.39×10⁻⁸, respectively, below the 0.1% quadrature target; see {{figref:rt0_spatial}}.

## Ansys port validation and capacity

<!-- id: S2.ansys.p1 -->
The historical two-dimensional seven-strand port test uses 704 elements, two voltage basis solutions, and a third actual ±20 A field solution. Common terminal voltage, complex strand currents, and independent power establish field–circuit consistency on that mesh; current results appear in {{figref:ansys_seven_current}}. Validation concerns one mesh. Subsequent fixed-mesh and precision attempts have not completed the required three-level checks, and the native three-dimensional J-expression probe has not produced an acceptable physical solution.

{{figref:ansys_limits}} summarizes the verified Ansys Student v252 limits: up to 2000 triangles in two dimensions, 64000 volume elements and 8000 surface elements in three dimensions, and at most four processor cores. Interface capability, licensed model size and numerical accuracy separately constrain executable validation. Actual terminations, curved STL geometry and large bundles with sufficient copper area require licensing and mesh capacity appropriate to the target geometry, followed by sensitivity and power-balance checks.
